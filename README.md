Albania has set an objective to join the European Union by 2030. This is a goal that requires sustained macroeconomic stability, institutional credibility, and resilience to potential economic shocks. The EU accession process places  emphasis on price and exchange rate stability, and economy robustness since these factors directly affect convergence with the EU economic standards.
My project evaluates Albania’s progress toward the objective by constructing an Economic Risk Index spanning 2010–2023 while capturing periods centered around major global and regional shocks. The index focuses on four key  channels that are vital to EU criteria:
- Foreign Exchange Risk (EU/ALL Exchange Rate)
- Inflation Risk 
- Energy Price Risk
- Tourism Risk 

By standardizing volatility across these indicators using z-scores, the index provides a relative measure of economic stress compared to Albania’s own long-run baseline. This allows for meaningful comparison across time.
The results suggest that while Albania experienced low and stable risk conditions in the early 2010s, subsequent periods which include the Greek debt crisis, the 2015 global oil price collapse, and the COVID-19 shock, introduced asymmetric vulnerabilities across sectors. Understanding how these risks evolved, and which components dominated during stress periods, is essential for assessing Albania’s readiness to meet EU accession requirements.
